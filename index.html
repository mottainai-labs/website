The blind spot

Households throw away food they forgot they owned, rebuy what is already in the cupboard, and cook the same few meals on rotation. These look like three problems. They are one problem: nobody knows what is actually in the house.

Every existing fix asks you to maintain the list yourself. Nobody does. The record has to build itself, or it does not exist.

One continuous record

We are building a single live inventory of the kitchen, kept current by what already happens there: what you buy, what you put away, what you use.

Everything people actually want sits downstream of that record. Recipes from what is on hand. Restocking before you run out. Nutrition measured over months instead of guessed at.

  • What comes inGroceries, receipts, something new on the shelf.
  • The recordOne live inventory that everything passes through.
  • What goes outCooking and depletion, which sets what comes in next.

The record is the hard part, and it is the part we are building first. Sprig is what it becomes.

もったいない

Mottainai is a Japanese expression of regret at waste, the sense that something still had use left in it. It is said about food, about objects, about time.

It is the whole company in one word. The work itself is unglamorous: knowing what you have, so that less of it is lost.
